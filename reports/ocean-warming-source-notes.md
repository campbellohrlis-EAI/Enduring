# Ocean-Warming Article Source Notes

**Draft:** *The Oceans Are Taking the Heat*  
**Proposed author:** Rob Kirkby, Founder and CEO, Enduring Advantage  
**Research review:** 1 October 2026

## Verified primary and authoritative sources

| Draft subject | Verification finding | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Ocean heat and Earth’s energy imbalance | WMO reports that more than 91% of excess heat is stored in the ocean. It records 2025 ocean heat content to 2,000 m as the highest since 1960, with the 2005–2025 warming rate more than twice the 1960–2005 rate. | [WMO, Earth’s climate swings increasingly out of balance](https://wmo.int/news/media-centre/earths-climate-swings-increasingly-out-of-balance) |
| Annual ocean energy uptake | WMO describes 2005–2025 ocean warming of about 11.0–12.2 zettajoules per year, around 18 times annual human energy use. | [WMO State of the Global Climate 2025](https://wmo.int/publication-series/state-of-global-climate/state-of-global-climate-2025) |
| Strong 2026–27 El Niño outlook | NOAA’s 10 September 2026 discussion says El Niño is strengthening, with a greater than 90% chance of a very strong event during Northern Hemisphere fall and winter 2026–27 and a 75% chance of historic strength in October–December. | [NOAA Climate Prediction Center ENSO Diagnostic Discussion](https://www.cpc.ncep.noaa.gov/products/analysis_monitoring/enso_advisory/ensodisc.shtml) |
| August 2026 temperatures and sea surface temperature | Copernicus records August 2026 as joint warmest on record, 1.65°C above the estimated pre-industrial baseline, and gives 21.11°C as the record daily global extra-polar sea-surface-temperature average. | [Copernicus Climate Change Service, August 2026](https://climate.copernicus.eu/copernicus-august-was-worlds-joint-hottest-month-record-pushing-global-temperatures-back-above) |
| Ocean state, acidity and sea ice | Copernicus Marine’s OSR10 reports 2025’s highest recorded ocean heat content, approximately 17% more acidic ocean conditions from 1985–2025, and Arctic monthly sea-ice decline of roughly 490,000 km² per decade from 1979–2025. | [Copernicus Marine, 10th Ocean State Report](https://marine.copernicus.eu/news/publication-of-10th-ocean-state-report) |
| Canadian El Niño impacts | ClimateData.ca supports the draft’s conditional framing: warmer winter conditions are likely in several regions, while western Canada can face lower snowpack, drought and wildfire risk. Effects vary by region. | [ClimateData.ca, El Niño and Canada 2026–27](https://climatedata.ca/news/what-the-developing-exceptional-el-nino-means-for-canada-in-2026-27/) |
| Tropical cyclones | NOAA GFDL supports the draft’s nuance that warming does not necessarily mean more storms, but may increase tropical cyclone intensity, rainfall and storm-surge exposure. | [NOAA GFDL, tropical cyclones and climate change](https://www.gfdl.noaa.gov/research_highlight/climate-change-is-probably-increasing-the-intensity-of-tropical-cyclones/) |
| Marine heatwaves and long-term ocean warming | IPCC AR6 reports an approximate doubling of marine heatwave frequency from 1982–2016, with greater intensity and duration since the 1980s, plus ocean warming irreversible over centuries to millennia. | [IPCC AR6 WG1 Chapter 9](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/chapter/chapter-9/) |
| Fisheries and coastal communities | NOAA Fisheries records that warming oceans affect species distribution and can disrupt fisheries, coastal businesses and communities. | [NOAA Fisheries, Understanding Our Changing Climate](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/insight/understanding-our-changing-climate) |
