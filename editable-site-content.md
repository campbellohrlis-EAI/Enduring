### No single project moves your bottom line. A fast run of the right ones does.
The curve is steep, so order matters. The size of the prize is why volume and speed matter just as much.

Every organization has thousands of candidate projects and they fall along a steeply declining curve. A few return far more than the rest, which is why sequence is worth so much. We find those few by screening the whole field first, then running the full analysis only on the standouts.

There is no silver bullet. No single project, however good, shows up in corporate profitability or market cap. Material impact comes from delivering a series of the right projects in the right order, quickly. So we identify the Next Best Thing, deliver it, reassess against the new data, and name the next one. The field keeps changing underneath you while you work. New technology, new incentives, new prices, new data of your own. That is why the ranking is never a one time exercise. We reassess whenever the data or the market moves, which is what keeps the answer right instead of letting it age.

Cost & Engagement

What does this cost, and how do you get paid?

We sell no equipment and take no referral fees. Our fee is a retainer plus a share of verified results. Coach and specialist time is passed through at cost. We only earn more when you save more.

What do we get if we stop after the Diagnostic?

Everything the Diagnostic produced is yours to keep: an initial baseline built from your available data, the opportunity map, and the one action that stands out with the numbers behind it. There is no lock-in. If you take the plan and execute it yourself or with another partner, you still leave with a clearer decision.

What happens after the first call?

The call is 30 minutes with a principal. We clarify the decision and tell you if there is likely a material opportunity. Next comes a situation assessment, data collection starts, and we identify low-risk first wins that can be proven with the data on hand. Ongoing work is a steady series of initiatives reported in dollars saved and environmental results.

Who usually hires you?

The CFO usually hires us. Operations, facilities, procurement and sustainability leaders weigh in on recommendations that affect them. We bring the right people in for each one. The outcome is better financial performance first, better sustainability performance close behind, with both reported continuously.

How much of our team's time will this take?

Less than you would expect. The heaviest lift is data access at the start: utility bills, operational records, and whatever environmental reporting you already have. After that, we do the analysis and come back to you with decisions, not homework. During implementation, your named Coach coordinates vendors and specialists so your team is not running another project on the side of their day jobs.

We are not a large enterprise. Is this still for us?

The approach scales down as well as up. Start with one operating group or one cost category across the business. The logic is the same at any size: establish the baseline, rank the opportunities, act on the strongest one, and let the results fund what comes next.

Results & Proof

How quickly will we see results?

It depends on the opportunity that ranks first, and we will not pretend otherwise. Some actions, such as rate structures, incentive capture and operational adjustments, can show results within months. Capital projects take longer. Speed is central to the model because the value comes from implementing the right sequence of projects, not from a single promise.

How do we know the savings are real and not consultant math?

Because they are measured, not asserted. Every completed project gets an M&V report. What if the analysis finds nothing worth doing?

Then we tell you that, and you walk away with an initial baseline and greater clarity. The purpose is a defensible next decision, including the decision not to proceed at that time.

How We Work

Who actually does the implementation work?

Specialist vendors do the physical work. That is their job and their expertise. Ours is different: we identify what should be done, in what order, and by whom, then coordinate the vendors, the financing, and the verification, and stay accountable for the outcome. We sit on your side of the table, outside the vendor playing field, which is precisely why we can select and manage vendors on merit.

Can we trust an AI-generated recommendation?

Not on its own, and neither do we. First, actio helps screen out what is not material and identify the next best move from the available data. What data do you need from us to get started?

Whatever you have. Utility bills, fuel and fleet records, waste invoices, building and equipment inventories, and prior audits or reports are useful starting points. What happens if we do not have good data?

You do not need a complete global data set before you begin. We start with the available data or funding path, take the quick wins first, and use the results to earn access to better data. The whole-field program comes later, once the data has compounded.

We already know what our biggest opportunity is?

Scope & Fit

Do you only work on buildings and energy?

No. Energy in buildings is often where the data is richest, but the playing field is much wider. Fleet and fuel switching, water, waste, supply chain, procurement, and sector-specific opportunities. The right starting point differs by organization. A municipality's priorities are not a manufacturer's, and a farm's are not a REIT's. That is exactly why we start wide. The bigger the field we search, the more likely your best opportunity is the true maximum, not just the best of a narrow list.

Why does our location matter to the analysis?

Because the same project produces different returns in different places. Utility and government funding programs, energy rates, insurance costs, and operating conditions all vary by jurisdiction, and they materially change payback. Higher energy rates make efficiency savings worth more; available incentives can transform a marginal project into a compelling one. Jurisdictional intelligence is one of the three pillars of our analysis, alongside your operational data and our vendor market data.

We mostly care about the financial return. Is that a problem?

Not at all. It is how we are built. A solution is only considered by us if it has a positive environmental impact and provides a financial return; within that field, we rank by overall business case. You never have to choose between an initiative that pays and an initiative that helps. The ones we recommend do both, and the business case is designed to survive policy changes.

Can you help us with climate risk, not just cost savings?

Yes, and it is a growing part of the work. Interconnected environmental pressures, including wildfires, flooding, drought, and supply chain disruption, are already showing up in insurance availability, input costs, and asset values. We help clients think strategically about exposure: where operations are located, how supply chains can be shortened, and which risks deserve action now. The same discipline applies: rank the exposures, act on the material ones first.

Do you work with First Nations communities?

We work within its governance and procurement, and community knowledge and decision authority stay where they are. Then we do what we do everywhere, which is find the single opportunity that returns most on the community’s own terms, deliver it, and come back for the next one. Circumstances differ by nation and we do not arrive with a view about which opportunity yours will be.

We already have sustainability advisors.
